Pixel Forge is a live, on-the-spot tech thumbnail design challenge. Teams are handed a short video on the day of the event and have to design a compelling 16:9 thumbnail for it within the time limit — no prep, no pre-made assets, just raw design instinct under pressure. It's a test of composition, visual storytelling, and how fast you can turn a rough clip into something people would actually click on.

SOFTWARE GUIDE

What each tool is for, so you know what to reach for

Ps — Adobe Photoshop
Image editing and compositing. Best for the actual thumbnail layout — cropping the frame, layering text and graphics, color grading, cutouts.
Pr — Adobe Premiere Pro
Video editing. Useful for scrubbing through the source video and pulling the exact frame or moment you want to build the thumbnail around.
Ae — Adobe After Effects
Motion graphics and effects. Handy if you want to add polish like glows, blur, or layered depth to text and graphic elements.

Any other offline design tool is also allowed — GIMP, Krita, Affinity Photo, whatever you're fastest in. Resources are downloaded in advance; no internet access during the round.
